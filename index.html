About

I'm a data scientist and engineer working at the intersection of statistical rigor and production systems.

My four years at IBM and TCS were spent inside large retail banking and telecommunications environments, where the cost of a broken pipeline is measured in missed SLAs and downstream analytics failures. I learned to design data quality frameworks, operate batch systems on Hadoop and Spark, and run zero-downtime migrations across hundreds of workflows. That work taught me something specific: the hardest part of machine learning in industry isn't the model, it's the data plumbing around it.

I'm now studying Data Science at Indiana University's Luddy School, focusing on applied machine learning, inference with statistics, and computer vision. My research applies self-supervised representation learning (ResNet-18, contrastive objectives) to egocentric infant video, and a separate project with Professor Madurima benchmarks retrieval architectures for biomedical genomics question answering.

Outside of academic work, I build finance-focused portfolio projects like value-at-risk models, GARCH volatility forecasting, and RAG systems for SEC filings to bridge toward my longer-term goal of quantitative and ML work at a top financial institution.
